# B2B Campaign Planning Framework

Before thinking about channels or creative, there's a sequencing problem most teams get wrong: they jump to brainstorming campaign themes before they've gathered the right inputs. The result is a theme that's either too narrow (product-led only) or too vague (brand direction without customer grounding).

This is the input-gathering and brainstorm process I use before any campaign theme gets locked.

---

## The Right Order

```
1. Gather inputs (Product Marketing + Brand Marketing)
        ↓
2. Develop campaign theme candidates
        ↓
3. Pressure-test: does it work across channels? Is it ownable?
        ↓
4. Align stakeholders in a shared brainstorm session
        ↓
5. Brief channels (Paid, Content, ABM, Events, Email)
```

---

## Step 1: Inputs Before Theme

### Product Marketing Input
The closest layer to the customer. Before any brainstorm, get answers to:
- Who is the target audience, and what segment are we prioritizing?
- What are their actual pain points — not what we assume, but what comes up in customer conversations and win/loss interviews?
- What is the value proposition, stated as a customer outcome?
- What are competitors saying? What do they own in the market?
- What is our differentiated proof point — the thing we can say that they can't?

### Brand Marketing Input
The strategic narrative layer. Before any brainstorm, get answers to:
- What is the brand's mission and core pillars?
- What is the external narrative direction this year?
- Are there brand guidelines or tone constraints that affect how we show up?
- What are we trying to shift in how buyers perceive us — not just awareness, but perception?

Both inputs must be on the table before you start generating theme ideas. Product Marketing without Brand gives you a campaign that's accurate but forgettable. Brand without Product Marketing gives you something that sounds good but doesn't connect to what buyers actually care about.

---

## Step 2: What Makes a Good Campaign Theme

A campaign theme is not a tagline or a slogan. The test I use:

**A good theme challenges an assumption the buyer currently holds.**

If the theme doesn't shift something — a belief, a mental model, a way of framing a problem — it isn't a theme. It's just a description.

Three additional tests:

| Test | What to ask |
|---|---|
| **Cross-channel** | Can this theme run in paid social, email, events, ABM, and content — and still feel like one campaign? |
| **Ownable** | Can you back this theme with product proof points that a competitor couldn't use? |
| **Audience-specific** | Does it land differently for a CFO vs. a VP of Marketing? Good themes flex. |

---

## Step 3: ABM Considerations

If you have an ABM (Account-Based Marketing) team focused on enterprise accounts, they need to be in the room early — not just handed a brief after the theme is set.

ABM requires themes that:
- Are broad enough to personalize by vertical, account, or persona
- Have specific enough direction that individual account messaging doesn't drift
- Support 1:1 or 1:few tactics (custom landing pages, direct mail, personalized sequences) in addition to 1:many paid campaigns

An ABM team briefed after the theme is finalized will often run a parallel track. An ABM team involved in the theme brainstorm will be a multiplier.

---

## Step 4: The Shared Brainstorm Session

This is where the sequencing pays off. Bring together:
- Product Marketing
- Brand Marketing
- Content
- Creative / Copywriting
- ABM
- Demand Gen / Campaign Manager

Each person comes with their input already processed into theme directions — not raw data. The session goal is to find the intersection: the angle that is customer-grounded (Product Marketing), narratively consistent (Brand), creatively executable (Creative/Copy), and scalable across segments (ABM).

**What makes this session work:**
- Everyone uses the same framework to evaluate ideas (see the three tests above)
- No one "presents" their team's preferred theme — everyone builds on each other
- The output is 2–3 candidate themes with a rationale, not a single "winner" forced in the room

The final theme decision should happen after the session, with a small group, using the cross-channel and ownable tests as the filter.

---

## Step 5: Channel Brief

Once the theme is locked, brief each channel with the same core message but channel-specific execution guidance:

| Channel | What the brief needs |
|---|---|
| Paid (LinkedIn, Google, Display) | Funnel stage focus, audience targeting parameters, content format per stage |
| Content | Pillar content that anchors the theme (whitepaper, report, blog series) |
| Email / Nurture | Sequence logic: how the theme shows up at different intent stages |
| ABM | Account-tier personalization plan, vertical-specific messaging variations |
| Events / Webinars | Speaking angle or co-host strategy aligned to the theme |

For more on the paid channel execution side, see [Channel & Content Guide](../retargeting/channel-and-content-guide.md).
